How McKinney Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
