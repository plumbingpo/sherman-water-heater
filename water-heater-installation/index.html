Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Plenty of Sherman, TX projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. Each of those jobs involves new gas piping, new vent routes, new circuits, or new water lines, and each one has to be planned before the first pipe is cut. Sherman Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Before any pipe is cut, we size the heater to your household. For tanks, first-hour rating counts more than gallons; for tankless, it is gallons per minute at your winter temperature rise. Next, we choose the spot together. A gas model has to vent outdoors and draw enough combustion air. A heat pump model has to have breathing room, temperatures above roughly 40 degrees, and a condensate drain. Electric tankless models often require a dedicated high-amperage circuit. And wherever a leak could damage finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. Clear choices and a firm price come before any work.

The install itself follows a checklist: gas piping sized, run, fitted with a sediment trap, and leak-tested, vents built to the manufacturer's size and pitch, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection between copper and steel. A full-size relief valve discharge line runs to a safe point, and any required expansion tank, drain pan, seismic straps, or permit is part of the job. To finish, we fill and purge the tank, test draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the thermostat near 120 degrees, and walk you through the new setup. What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a water heater from scratch involves gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. The plumbing side needs a correctly rated relief valve and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check before we leave. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
