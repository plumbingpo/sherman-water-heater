Most water heaters do not simply wear out; they rust out or scale up, and both problems can be held off for years. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Sherman, TX, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

Each visit follows the same thorough checklist. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is swapped when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater

Act Before It Gets Worse

Most water heater failures start small: a popping sound from sediment, a damp spot under the drain valve, or a shower that goes lukewarm a few minutes sooner each week. If you notice any of these signs, contact Sherman Water Heater at (903) 202-2373 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

There is a short list of things you can do yourself: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

We come prepared for what an older heater throws at us: replacement drain valves, anode rods sized for your tank, flexible anodes for tight clearances, T&P valves, and meters for testing elements and thermostats. For tankless systems, we bring the pump and descaling setup. Just as important, we know what to look for, from early corrosion at the fittings to a thermocouple starting to weaken, so you can plan a repair before it becomes a failure. The goal is simple: a heater that lasts longer, runs more efficiently, and does not surprise you with a leak.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
