The majority of tankless breakdowns announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see week after week in Sherman, TX.

Error codes are a starting point, not a diagnosis, and we treat them that way on every Sherman call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Sherman Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which reveals a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

There are two reasons tankless repair belongs with a professional: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. Errors with either type can be serious.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
